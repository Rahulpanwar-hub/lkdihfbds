🧬 Why Carbon Forms Millions of Compounds

1. Catenation (Self-Linking):
Carbon forms strong covalent C—C bonds with other carbon atoms to create long straight chains, branched chains, and closed rings.
2. Tetravalency (Valency = 4):
With 4 valence electrons, Carbon bonds with 4 monovalent atoms (H, Cl) or forms multiple bonds with O, N, and S.
3. Small Atomic Size:
Carbon's nucleus holds shared electron pairs tightly, making C—C and C—H bonds exceptionally stable.

📚 Why Carbon Doesn't Form Ionic Bonds

Carbon has atomic number Z = 6 (electronic configuration 2, 4). To attain noble gas configuration:

❌ Cannot gain 4 electrons (C⁴⁻ anion):
It is energetically difficult for a nucleus with only 6 protons to hold onto 10 electrons (4 extra electrons).
❌ Cannot lose 4 electrons (C⁴⁺ cation):
Removing 4 electrons requires a massive amount of ionization energy, leaving behind a tiny cation with 6 protons holding just 2 electrons.

✓ Solution: Carbon shares its 4 valence electrons to form Covalent Bonds!

📖 Key NCERT Organic Concepts

Homologous Series Rule:
Successive members differ by a —CH₂— unit and a molecular mass of 14 u. They share the same functional group and chemical properties, while boiling/melting points increase with molecular mass.
Combustion & Addition Reactions:
• Saturated (Alkanes): Burn with a clean blue flame; undergo substitution with $\text{Cl}_2$ in sunlight.
• Unsaturated (Alkenes/Alkynes): Burn with a yellow sooty flame; undergo Addition Reaction ($\text{H}_2$ + Ni catalyst) to form saturated fats/alkanes.
